I’m a Gen X engineer in my early 50s. I think a lot of us have lived most of our lives assuming we were indestructible. We’d get through the workday, fix whatever broke, and get around to taking care of ourselves when things slowed down.

Funny how things never slow down.

A friend of mine just had open-heart surgery at 54. It came out of the blue. Another friend, someone I’ve known since high school, just had colon cancer. I still think of us as the people we were in high school. Hearing that news will mess with your sense of time a little.

Then there’s my own stuff. I’ve dealt with vestibular issues and had a thyroid scare. Having trouble with your balance makes you appreciate how much you took it for granted. And a scare is still a scare while you’re waiting to find out what it means.

We aren’t indestructible. I know that sounds obvious. I’m in my early 50s and apparently still need the reminder.

## Make the appointment

I can put a reminder on my calendar to update a server. I can also manage to spend six months saying, “Yeah, I need to call the doctor.” If that sounds familiar, here are a few things worth talking about at your next visit.

- **Colon cancer screening.** I grew up hearing “get a colonoscopy at 50.” The current recommendation starts screening at **45** for most adults at average risk. If you’re already in your 50s and haven’t done it, make the call. Ask about a colonoscopy or a stool-based screening option, and tell your doctor if colon cancer runs in your family. [U.S. Preventive Services Task Force guidance](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/colorectal-cancer-screening)
- **Prostate and testicular health.** Guys, ask when it makes sense to discuss a PSA blood test, especially if you have a family history of prostate cancer. The U.S. Preventive Services Task Force recommends an individual decision about PSA screening from 55 to 69. And if you notice a new testicular lump or swelling, get it checked. Routine testicular cancer screening isn’t recommended for people without symptoms. [Prostate screening guidance](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/prostate-cancer-screening) · [Testicular screening guidance](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/testicular-cancer-screening)
- **Breast screening and other women’s health.** I’m less knowledgeable here, so I’ll stick to what I looked up. The Task Force recommends a mammogram every two years from 40 through 74 for women at average risk. Ask what schedule fits your history, whether you’re due for cervical screening, and bring up menopause symptoms if they’re affecting your life. [Breast screening guidance](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/breast-cancer-screening) · [Cervical screening guidance](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/cervical-cancer-screening)
- **Heart and circulation.** Ask about your blood pressure, cholesterol, and family history. Tell your doctor if you’ve noticed changes when you exercise or if your calves hurt when you walk. A cardiac stress test or a test for peripheral artery disease, such as comparing blood pressure at your ankle and arm, may make sense depending on what’s going on. Turning 50 by itself doesn’t put either test on everybody’s list. [Heart screening guidance](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/cardiovascular-disease-risk-screening-with-electrocardiography) · [Peripheral artery disease screening guidance](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/peripheral-artery-disease-in-adults-screening-with-the-ankle-brachial-index)

If you haven’t been in for a while, you don’t need to have all of this figured out before you call. Make the appointment. Write down what’s been bothering you, what runs in the family, and what you keep forgetting to ask. Bring the list. I know how easy it is to get to “Anything else?” and suddenly forget the one thing I meant to mention.

We all want to be around for the people we care about. For some of us, that’s our kids. For others, it’s our friends and their kids. I want to see what they’re all up to ten or twenty years from now. That’s a pretty good reason to spend twenty minutes on hold making an appointment.

The work will still be there when you get back.
