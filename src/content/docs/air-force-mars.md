I'm a member of the **65th Communications Group, 6th Communications Wing, Air Force MARS**, and have served as an operator since March 2023. This page summarizes the program and what it takes to join. The authoritative source is always [afmars.com](https://afmars.com), so check it before you apply.

## What Air Force MARS is

Air Force MARS (Military Auxiliary Radio System) is a **Department of Defense recognized auxiliary**. According to the AFMARS General Information sheet, it is charged by DoD Instruction 4650.02 to provide high frequency (HF) radio communications support to the DoD through FCC-licensed volunteer radio operators, as directed by the Services and coordinated within the DoD.

In practice that means:

- On-air operations happen mainly on **HF nets and exercises**.
- Members are tasked by the military chain of command.
- Nets run on **upper sideband (USB)** on military frequencies, which are outside the normal amateur allocations.
- Message traffic is handled with the **M110A** digital protocol, so your station must send and receive digital modes.
- Air Force MARS is a DoD auxiliary. It does **not** support ARES, RACES or other agencies as MARS unless specifically tasked.

## Requirements to join

Meet all of these before you submit an application:

| Requirement | Details |
|---|---|
| Citizenship | US citizen or legal resident alien |
| Age | 18 or older |
| License | FCC-issued amateur radio license, any class |
| HF station | Operational HF radio and antenna capable of operating from 2.0 to 30 MHz, with at least 100 W PEP SSB output. This generally requires a modification to the radio. Most operations are on the 40 m to 80 m range. |
| Computer | Windows 10 or 11 PC |
| Digital modes | Able to transmit and receive digital modes. VOX keying is still supported, but members are encouraged to move to hardware or CAT keying. |
| Internet | Needed for many AFMARS interactions and for software and instructions |

Air Force MARS does not provide or perform modifications to your radio. If you can't do the modification yourself, use a reputable service center. For a wide-band antenna option, the program's information sheet points to [hflink.com/antenna](https://hflink.com/antenna/).

## How to apply

1. Download the AFMARS application (linked from the **Join AFMARS** button at [afmars.com](https://afmars.com)) and **save it to your desktop before filling it out**.
2. Complete the fillable PDF, then email it **as a PDF attachment**, along with a reference copy of your FCC license, to [hqccc.afmars@us.af.mil](mailto:hqccc.afmars@us.af.mil). Other file formats are returned.
3. Your application is reviewed and sent to your Wing or Group Commander, who contacts you for a personal interview.
4. You are accepted **for training** when you receive your **training call sign**. When you finish all training requirements, you move from training status to full member status.

By signing the application you certify that the information on the forms is accurate and truthful. You can look up your FCC license with the [FCC ULS license search](https://wireless2.fcc.gov/UlsApp/UlsSearch/searchLicense.jsp).

## Training

You have up to **270 days** from receiving your training call sign to finish. Expect to spend about **four to five hours per week**. Your Wing Training Officer runs the training, which includes:

- Serving as **Net Control Station (NCS)** during a net. This is a core task and must be completed.
- Composing, sending and receiving digital messages to show you can use the software.
- An open-book test at the end of the course.
- Computer-based training (CBT) courses, repeated every two years.
- Military protocol and procedure on military frequencies. Strict adherence is required.

## Staying qualified

- Be operational in your primary assignment, generally your Wing Traffic Net, for **18 hours per calendar quarter**.
- Take part in **two quarterly communications exercises** each year.
- Stay current on recurring computer-based training.

## Software you receive

The program furnishes its message handling application (the **Network Operations Software System, NOSS**), a software modem, and an offline encryption/decryption application. Updates and backups are coordinated over the internet.

## Things to consider first

- Can you give the time for initial training and for the continuing hours?
- Can your radio and antenna operate on military frequencies outside the amateur bands, with 100 W PEP SSB or more?
- Are you comfortable following strict military operating procedures?

MARS frequencies and operating procedures are for members, so this site doesn't publish operational details.
