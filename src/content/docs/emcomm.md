Emergency communications (EMCOMM) is about keeping people connected when normal infrastructure fails. This page covers the programs and skills I train in, from my [Air Force MARS](/docs/air-force-mars/) membership to local volunteer work.

## Where I serve

### Texas RACES

I'm a member of **Texas RACES** (Radio Amateur Civil Emergency Service), the volunteer amateur radio service that supports emergency management agencies in Texas. RACES operates under the direction of emergency management officials, so training and exercises follow their procedures.

### SkyWARN

I've completed **SkyWARN Basic and Advanced training**. SkyWARN is the National Weather Service's trained storm-spotter program. Spotters report severe weather such as tornadoes, hail, damaging wind and flooding so forecasters can warn the public faster. Amateur radio operators often relay those reports during active weather.

### New Braunfels CERT Watch Tower Program

I take part in the **New Braunfels CERT Watch Tower Program**. Watch Tower volunteers monitor for flooding and weather events and report what they see back to the City and County Emergency Operations Center (EOC), giving emergency managers eyes on the ground during fast-changing conditions. See [New Braunfels CERT](/docs/new-braunfels-cert/) for the team and my other roles with it.

### Weather observing

Good spotter reports start with good data, so I also run my own weather station and have contributed observations for almost 30 years:

- **Station AP086**, reporting to [MesoWest](https://mesowest.utah.edu) and the Citizen Weather Observer Program (CWOP). It has been on the air almost 30 years, since before CWOP was established.
- **APRS:** [N5SNT-2](https://aprs.fi/info/a/N5SNT-2)
- **Weather Underground:** [KTXNEWBR1](https://www.wunderground.com/dashboard/pws/KTXNEWBR1)
- **CoCoRaHS:** I'm a member of the [Community Collaborative Rain, Hail & Snow Network](https://www.cocorahs.org), whose volunteers measure and report daily precipitation.

## Skills and tools

### Winlink (Packet and VARA)

**Winlink** is a global radio email system that works without the internet. Messages travel by radio to gateway stations, which connect to the Winlink network. I use both:

- **Packet** on VHF/UHF, for local and regional messages through nearby gateways. It is a good fit for short-range emergency traffic.
- **VARA**, a high-performance modem, for faster Winlink sessions over HF and VHF/UHF FM.

Winlink is widely used in emergencies for formal, written messages such as status reports, resource requests and welfare traffic.

### fldigi net monitoring

I monitor HF digital-mode nets with **fldigi**, using a receive-only setup that can follow nets on a schedule. My [FLDigi Net Scheduler](/projects/fldigi-net-scheduler/) retunes fldigi on a UTC schedule and can record the net, and the [KiwiSDR Remote Client](/projects/kiwisdr-remote/) lets me listen through a remote receiver, including [my own](http://newbraunfels.proxy.kiwisdr.com:8073). See [HF digital modes](/docs/hf-digital-modes/) for how to get started.

## Mesh networking

- **Meshtastic:** low-power LoRa mesh messaging. No license is required for typical use.
- **MeshCore:** another LoRa mesh messaging system, with a different network design.
- **AREDN:** high-power amateur-radio mesh built on Wi-Fi hardware ("ham wifi"). A license is required.

## Go-kit checklist

- Radio, antenna and coax, plus a spare battery or power source
- Pencil, paper and a printed frequency list
- Headlamp, water, food and any needed medications
- Local contacts and written net procedures
